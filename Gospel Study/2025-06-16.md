### **1. Locations of Electrons in an Atom (Electron "Bands")**

When we talk about the "location" of electrons in an atom, we're really discussing **quantum probability distributions**, not fixed paths like planets orbiting the Sun.

#### a. **Electron "Bands" = Energy Levels = Shells**

Electrons in atoms occupy **energy levels (or shells)**, which are often referred to as **bands** in solid-state or semiconductor physics. Each shell is defined by a **principal quantum number**, `n = 1, 2, 3, ...`, and contains one or more **orbitals**, each with a different shape and energy.

Each energy level (band) can be described as:

- **n = 1** → Closest to nucleus, lowest energy (called the K shell)
    
- **n = 2** → Next level up (L shell)
    
- And so on...
    

Electrons don't have precise locations; instead, **quantum mechanics** tells us the probability of finding them in certain regions. These are the **orbitals**—s, p, d, f, etc.—within each shell.

#### b. **How Electron Positions Are Calculated**

We calculate electron distributions using the **Schrödinger equation**, which solves for **wavefunctions** (ψ). The **square of the wavefunction**, |ψ|², gives the **probability density** of finding an electron at a certain point in space.

Each electron’s state is defined by four quantum numbers:

|Quantum Number|Symbol|Meaning|
|---|---|---|
|Principal|`n`|Shell/energy level|
|Angular Momentum|`l`|Orbital type (s, p, d, f...)|
|Magnetic|`m_l`|Orientation of orbital|
|Spin|`m_s`|Electron spin (+½ or -½)|

These determine the "band" and shape/size of the region where the electron is likely to be found.

### **2. Electron Velocity in Outer Bands**

This is subtle because "velocity" in quantum mechanics is not as straightforward as in classical mechanics. But we can talk about trends.

#### a. **Kinetic Energy and Velocity Relationship**

In classical terms:

KE=12mv2KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2KE=21​mv2

In atoms:

- **Inner electrons** (closer to the nucleus) have **stronger attraction** to the positively charged nucleus → **higher binding energy** → **higher kinetic energy** (to balance potential energy).
    
- **Outer electrons** are **less tightly bound**, and have **lower kinetic energy**.
    

So, **classically**, **outer electrons move more slowly** than inner electrons.

#### b. **Quantum Interpretation**

Quantum mechanically:

- Electrons are not particles orbiting at specific speeds. But we can still associate an average kinetic energy with each orbital.
    
- Electrons in **lower energy levels** (inner shells) have **higher average kinetic energies** due to stronger nuclear attraction.
    
- Electrons in **outer shells** (higher `n`) are more spread out and have **lower average kinetic energy** → implying **lower "effective" velocities**.
    

---

### **Summary**

- **Electron locations** are described by orbitals (solutions to the Schrödinger equation), not fixed paths.
    
- Electrons occupy discrete **energy bands (shells)** with increasing size and energy as `n` increases.
    
- **Outer electrons move more slowly on average** because they are less tightly bound and have lower kinetic energy.
